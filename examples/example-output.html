2.2 Epidemic Curves & Outbreak Investigation

  • Point-source: single sharp peak. Continuous common-source: prolonged plateau. Propagated (person-to-person): successive peaks ~1 incubation period apart.
Index caseFirst case brought to investigator≠ primary case (first infecter, may be undetected).

2.3 Carriers, Reservoirs & Transmission

CarrierDescription
IncubatorySheds BEFORE symptoms
ConvalescentSheds DURING recovery (typhoid, diphtheria)
HealthyNever develops disease but harbours agent
ChronicSheds for months/years (Typhoid Mary, HBV)

Reservoir vs Source: Reservoir = natural habitat where agent lives/multiplies. Source = person/animal/object from which agent passes to host. Tetanus: reservoir=soil; source=contaminated wound/instrument.
  • Zoonoses — animal reservoir → human: rabies, plague, brucellosis. Anthroponoses — human-only: typhoid, measles. Sapronoses — environmental (soil): tetanus. Animal reservoir makes eradication harder.
FomiteInanimate object (doorknob, towel)Vehicle=inanimate medium (food, water). Vector=living arthropod.
Serial intervalSymptom onset (primary) → symptom onset (secondary)Generation time=infection→infection; Incubation=infection→symptom; Latent=infection→infectiousness.

2.4 Immunity & Screening

  • Active: host's own response (infection/vaccination), slow, durable/lifelong. Passive: ready-made antibodies, immediate, short-lived.
ScreeningDescription
MassWhole population (unselected)
SelectiveOnly high-risk groups
MultiphasicSeveral tests on ONE occasion
OpportunisticDuring unrelated consultation
Sensitivity = TP/(TP+FN)  |  Specificity = TN/(TN+FP)  |  PPV = TP/(TP+FP)  |  NPV = TN/(TN+FN)
Worked example (Q66): 80 TP, 20 FN, 855 TN, 45 FP → Sens=80/100=80%; Spec=855/900=95%; PPV=80/125=64%.
SnNout / SpPin: high SEN + Negative test = rule OUT; high SPEC + Positive = rule IN.
  • Length bias: slow-progressing cases have longer detectable phase → over-represented → falsely improved outcomes.
  • Lead-time bias: earlier diagnosis appears to prolong survival without postponing death.
  • Reliability = repeatability/precision. Validity = measures what it should.

2.5 Mortality Measures

3. Sterilization, Disinfection & Vector Control

3.1 Physical Sterilization

Hot Air OvenTime
160°C1 hr
170°C30 min
150°C150 min
Autoclave: 121°C / 15 min / 15 psi. Bowie-Dick test: daily air-removal & steam-penetration test in vacuum autoclave (not a sterility test).
  • Gamma/X-rays = ionizing, penetrating → disposable syringes, sutures, plastics. Cobalt-60 = standard commercial gamma source.
  • UV light = non-ionizing, POOR penetration → air/surface disinfection only.

3.2 Chemical Sterilization & Disinfection

AgentPrincipal Use
Glutaraldehyde 2%Cold sterilization of endoscopes
Ethylene oxide (EtO)Heat-labile plastics, catheters, endoscopes (alkylates proteins/DNA at low temp)
Formaldehyde vapourFumigation of operation theatres
Povidone-iodineSkin antiseptic
  • Coal-tar phenolics: Cresol (stronger than phenol); Lysol = soapy emulsion of cresol; work in presence of organic matter; NOT safe on open wounds (tissue-irritant).
  • Concurrent disinfection: during illness on infected discharges. Terminal: after patient removal/recovery. Pre-onset = prophylactic (not terminal).
  • Syringes: disposable plastic → gamma; reusable glass → hot air/autoclave; metal needles → autoclave. Red heat destroys plastic.

4.6 Vegetable Drugs, Galenicals & Drug Properties

  • Vegetable drugs come from roots, bark, leaves, seeds, fruits, flowers, entire plants (not only leaves/bark). Fixed oils = glycerol esters; volatile oils evaporate; alkaloids = N-containing plant bases.
  • Galenicals = extracts of natural drugs (tinctures, fluid extracts, infusions). E.g., tincture of opium (laudanum). Aspirin/adrenaline/paracetamol syrup = pure single-entity.
  • TI ↑ = wider safety margin. Potency = amount needed (lower dose = more potent). Efficacy = max achievable effect. Zero oral bioavailability (aminoglycosides, heparin) doesn't prevent parenteral use.

5. Anatomy & Physiology Essentials

5.1 Eye Layers & Nerve Conduction

  • Sclera — outer, tough, white, fibrous. Choroid — middle, vascular, pigmented. Retina — innermost, light-sensitive. Conjunctiva — thin covering front of sclera.
  • Conduction speed ↑ with: myelin sheath, larger axon diameter, higher temperature. ↓ with: more synapses (each adds delay).
  • Repolarization: K⁺ efflux (Na⁺ channels inactivate, voltage-gated K⁺ open). Depolarization = Na⁺ influx.

5.2 Blood Groups (AB × O)

AB parent → IA or IB; O parent (ii) → i only. Children: IAi (A) or IBi (B) — never AB or O. Classic exception to "direct transmission" of parental groups.

5.3 Cardiovascular Physiology

TermDefinition
PreloadVentricular stretch from end-diastolic filling
AfterloadResistance ventricle must overcome to eject blood
ComplianceVolume change per unit pressure change
TPROverall resistance of systemic vessels
Pulse pressureSystolic − Diastolic (e.g., 120−80=40)
JVPReflects right atrial / central venous pressure
Velocity slowest in capillaries — enormous combined cross-sectional area; velocity ∝ 1/area. Allows time for exchange.
SV = EDV − ESV  |  EF = SV / EDV

6. Pharmaceutics – Weighing, Extraction, Mixing & Drying

6.1 Balance & Weighing

  • Sensitivity = min weight causing one-division pointer deflection. Dispensing (Class B) ≈ 6 mg; Analytical (Class A) ≈ 0.1 mg.
Minimum Weighable Quantity (MWQ) = (Sensitivity Requirement × 100) / Acceptable % error
Q128: SR=6 mg, error=5% → MWQ=(6×100)/5 = 120 mg. Weighing 300 mg → error=(6/300)×100 = 2% (safe).
Q107: 120 mg atropine, Class A (SR=0.1 mg) → %error=(0.1/120)×100 ≈ 0.083%.
  • Volumetric (transfer) pipette — most accurate for single fixed volume. Read bottom of meniscus at eye level for transparent liquids; TOP for opaque (mercury, convex).

6.2 Size Reduction (Comminution)

TechniqueMechanism
TriturationMortar & pestle, rotary grinding
LevigationGrind with small quantity of INSOLUBLE liquid (mineral oil, glycerin) → paste
Pulverization by interventionGummy/resinous drug dissolved in volatile solvent → evaporate → fine particles
CuttingSharp blades for fibrous/tough materials (roots, barks)

6.4 Mixing & Blending

  • Geometric dilution — uniform distribution of potent drug in large diluent (progressively double). Tumbling mixers (V-blender, double cone) by rotation. Trituration mixes + reduces size. Spatulation — small qty only.

6.5 Emulsification Methods

Continental (Dry Gum), Oil:Water:Gum ratio: Fixed oil = 4:2:1; Volatile oil = 2:2:1; Mineral oil = 3:2:1. Gum triturated with oil first, then water added ALL AT ONCE.
Q113: 200 mL fixed oil → Gum = 200/4 = 50 g; Water = 200/2 = 100 mL.
  • English (Wet Gum): gum dissolved in water first → mucilage; oil added IN SMALL PORTIONS with continuous trituration.
  • Bottle (Forbes): for volatile & mineral oils (low viscosity); oil + acacia shaken dry, then water in portions.
Continental=Dry gum + water all at once. English=Wet gum + oil in portions.

6.7 Distillation, Drying, Crystallization & Fusion

DistillationWhen to Use
SimpleBoiling point difference > 25°C
FractionalClosely boiling mixtures (fractionating column)
SteamHeat-sensitive, water-immiscible substances (below normal BP)
VacuumThermolabile liquids (reduced pressure LOWERS BP)
DryerApplication
Tray dryerBulk crude drugs & chemicals (hot air on trays)
Spray dryerLiquid feeds → dry powder rapidly
Freeze dryer (lyophilization)Heat-sensitive biologicals/vaccines/proteins — sublimation under reduced pressure
Fluidized bed dryerGranules suspended in upward hot air — fast, uniform (tablet granulation)
  • Crystallization: dissolve in hot solvent → supersaturate on cooling. Seeding = nucleation sites. Rapid cooling → small crystals; slow cooling → few, larger, purer crystals.
  • Fusion: melting solids together by heat (ointments, suppositories). Add ingredients in descending order of MP.
