1. Sterilization & Disinfection — Methods

1.1 Sterilization vs Disinfection — the core divide

Sterilization = destruction of ALL microbes incl. bacterial spores. Disinfection = kills vegetative forms but NOT reliably spores.
Why is boiling (100°C, 10–20 min) only disinfection, not sterilization?Cannot reliably kill bacterial endosporesSpores of Clostridium & Bacillus survive hours of boiling; need >100°C under pressure (autoclave).

1.2 Heat methods — moist vs dry

FeatureMoist heat (steam)Dry heat
Kill mechanismProtein coagulation/denaturationOxidation (charring cell constituents)
Energy neededLower (latent heat of steam)Higher → higher temp + longer time
Temp / time121°C, 15 min, 15 psi (autoclave)160–170°C; hot air oven 160°C / 60 min
Moist beats dry — steam's latent heat transfers energy straight to proteins, so lower temp + shorter time suffice than dry-heat oxidation.

Moist heat family (all moist EXCEPT incineration, which is dry)

MethodConditionUse / principle
Autoclave121°C / 15 min / 15 psiStandard steam sterilization
Tyndallization (intermittent)100°C / 30 min × 3 successive daysSpores germinate between cycles (incubate 37°C), killed as vegetative form next cycle
InspissationSteam, coagulates egg mediaSerum/egg-based media (e.g. LJ)
Pasteurization — holder63°C / 30 minThermolabile liquids
Pasteurization — HTST/flash72°C / 15 s → rapid cool <10°CShort exposure minimises thermal degradation
Boiling100°C / 10–20 minDisinfection only (no spores)

Dry heat & heat-sensitive materials

Why sterilize glass syringes in a hot-air oven, not an autoclave?Dry heat avoids alkaline-condensate damageRepeated autoclaving etches/frosts glass → ruins barrel–plunger fit. Dry heat 160°C / 60 min preferred.
Method that DESTROYS rubber goodsHot air oven 160°C (melts/degrades)Rubber OK by: autoclave, gamma, EO gas, H₂O₂ plasma.

1.3 Loading the autoclave

  • Loose arrangement → allows steam penetration. Drain clear → gravity air displacement.
  • Mixed loads: textiles on TOP (absorb dripping condensate), hard goods BELOW.
  • Liquid containers: fill only to 2/3 capacity (expansion / prevent boil-over) — NOT full.

2. Sterilization Kinetics & SAL

2.1 D-value, Z-value, F₀, SAL

TermMeaning
D-valueTime for 1 log (90%) reduction at given temp
Z-valueTemp change for 10× change in D-value; ≈ 10°C for G. stearothermophilus (moist heat)
SALSterility Assurance Level = probability of a surviving / non-sterile unit
log reductions = exposure time ÷ D-value  |  SAL = log(initial bioburden) − total log reductions

2.2 Pharmacopoeial SAL targets

SAL for terminally sterilized parenterals10⁻⁶ (≤1 non-sterile unit per million)Aseptically processed products may accept 10⁻³ due to process limits.

2.3 Worked examples

SAL calc: D₁₂₁ = 1.5 min, initial spores = 10⁶, exposure 15 min. Log reductions = 15÷1.5 = 10. SAL = 6 − 10 = −4 → 10⁻⁴. (12D = 18 min would give the pharmacopoeial 10⁻⁶.)
Dry-heat tunnel calc: exposure = length ÷ belt speed = 150 cm ÷ 10 cm/min = 15 min. Endotoxin reduction = log(10 000 ÷ 0.25) = log(40 000) = 4.6 log. Depyrogenation needs ≥3 log → PASS.

4. Chemical Agents & Disinfectants

4.1 Mechanisms of action

AgentClassMechanism
Peracetic acidOxidizerDisrupts sulfhydryl (–SH) & disulfide bonds
ChlorhexidineBiguanideDisrupts cell membrane
Ethylene oxide (EO)Alkylating gasAlkylates amino/carboxyl/hydroxyl groups of protein & DNA
FormaldehydeAlkylatingCross-links amino groups of proteins & nucleic acids
H₂O₂ vapor/plasmaOxidizerGenerates hydroxyl free radicals; STERRAD, <50°C, heat-sensitive items

4.2 Disinfectant spectrum & uses

Class of QACs (benzalkonium chloride)Low-level disinfectantKills vegetative bacteria, some fungi, enveloped viruses only. Ineffective vs mycobacteria, non-enveloped viruses, spores.
Lysol (cresol + soap) 3–5% — used for?Non-critical surfaces only (floors, walls, furniture)Too toxic/corrosive for semi-critical or critical items.
Formaldehyde gas (from paraformaldehyde) main useFumigation of cleanrooms, LAF hoods, OTsNeeds sealed space; neutralise with ammonia before reuse.

4.3 Alcohols

  • Ethanol 70% > isopropanol 70% against non-enveloped viruses.
  • Act by denaturing proteins + dissolving lipids; cannot penetrate spore coats.
  • Absolute alcohol dehydrates surface too fast → coagulated protein layer blocks further penetration → 70% is optimal.

4.4 Radiation sterilization

  • Electron beam: lower penetration (few cm) but high dose rate → fast processing.
  • Standard sterilizing dose = 25 kGy.
  • Ionizing radiation → free radicals (hydroxyl from water radiolysis) damage DNA.
  • Infrared = non-ionizing thermal, NOT in the gamma/e-beam class.

6. Biomedical Waste Management

Culture plates / microbial stocks awaiting autoclave → which container?Yellow non-chlorinated bags, biohazard symbolCategory 1 (Yellow); pre-treat by autoclave/microwave, then incinerate.
Correct sharps disposal practicesPuncture-proof biohazard containers; autoclave first; white translucent tamper-proof lidsNEVER manually recap needles (needlestick risk); phenolic soak not standard for sharps.
Contaminated media disposal: decontaminate ALL media (autoclave 121°C); hypochlorite OK for liquid waste; solid plates → incineration. Yellow bags carry pathological/infectious waste in this scheme.

8. Dale's Cone & Learning Retention

10–20–30–50–70–90 retention ladder — Read 10, Hear 20, See 30, Hear+See 50, Say 70, Do 90.
ActivityRetained
Read10%
Hear20%
See (demonstration alone)30%
Hear + See (demonstration + lecture)50%
Say70%
Do actively (perform activity)90%
Common trap: "Demonstration + Lecture = 50%" DOES align (hear+see). Demonstration alone would be 30%.
Efficiency calc: efficiency = behaviour change ÷ knowledge gain. 8/30=0.27 · 35/60=0.58 · 12/45=0.27 · 40/55=0.73 (highest) → participatory community involvement most effective.

10. HE Ethics, Planning & Concepts

10.1 Ethical principles & educator attributes

NOT an ethical principle of HECoercion for complianceCore principles: autonomy, beneficence, non-maleficence, justice, truthfulness, cultural sensitivity.
"Beneficence" meansActing in best interest/welfare of people (doing good)Non-maleficence = avoid harm; justice = fairness; autonomy = respect choice.
Effective educator attributesCultural competence, empathy, communication, rapport, credibilityAuthoritarian approach is NOT an attribute (contrary to participatory philosophy).
Core HE learning principleParticipatory learningActive involvement → better retention & sustainable behaviour change.

10.2 Programme planning

4-step cycle: (1) Need assessment [FIRST step] → (2) Set objectives / plan → (3) Implement & monitor → (4) Evaluate + follow-up. Evaluation is ONGOING (formative + summative), not only at the end.
Health literacyCapacity to obtain, process & understand basic health info to make decisionsGoes beyond simple reading ability.

12. Inventory Control & Formulas

12.1 Stock level & EOQ formulas

EOQ = √(2DS/H)  |  Reorder Level = Max consumption × Max lead time
Min stock = ROL − (Normal consumption × Normal lead time)
Max stock = ROL + Reorder Qty − (Min consumption × Min lead time)

D = annual demand, S = ordering cost/order, H = carrying cost/unit/yr.

12.2 Worked EOQ examples

EOQ calc A: D=10 000, S=100, H=25%×40=10 → EOQ=√(2·10000·100/10)=√200000 ≈ 447 units.
EOQ calc B: D=5000, S=200, H=10 → EOQ=√(2·5000·200/10)=√200000 ≈ 447 units.

12.3 Inventory analysis methods

MethodBasis
ABC (Always Better Control)Consumption VALUE — A ≈ 70% value / 10% items
VEDCriticality — Vital, Essential, Desirable
FSNMovement — Fast, Slow, Non-moving
SDEProcurement difficulty — Scarce, Difficult, Easy
ABC = money, VED = criticality, FSN = speed, SDE = supply difficulty.

12.4 Near-expiry & rate contracts

  • Near-expiry: alert 3–6 months ahead; segregate + distinct labels; destroy per procedure; transfer to high-consumption areas. (Return-to-supplier depends on contract, not universal.)
  • DGS&D = Directorate General of Supplies & Disposals; rate contracts fix prices for a period, uniform pricing across govt institutions, standard quality specs. (Individual indents/supply orders STILL required.)
