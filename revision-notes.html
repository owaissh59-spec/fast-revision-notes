1. Tray Composition

Tray typeApprox. instrument countTypical contents
Basic / minor procedures15–25Scalpel handle, tissue forceps, artery forceps, scissors, needle holder, retractors, drapes
Major abdominal60–100Scalpels, multiple forceps sizes, scissors, retractors (Balfour, Deaver), bowel clamps, needle holders, suction tips + procedure-specific extras
Major, all specialties50–100+Varies with the operation
"Minor 20, Major 80" — anchor the midpoints; the ranges fall out either side.

2. General Surgery Core Set

2.1 The workhorse groups

GroupMembersFunction
Haemostatic forcepsSpencer Wells, Crile, Mosquito (finest)Clamp bleeding vessels
Tissue forcepsAllis (interlocking teeth, crushing), Babcock (fenestrated, atraumatic)Grasp & hold tissue / skin edges
Scalpel handles#3 → blades 10–15  |  #4 → blades 20–25Carry the cutting blade
Handle-to-blade: small handle #3 takes the teens (10–15); big handle #4 takes the twenties (20–25).

2.2 Scissors family

ScissorsBuildUse
MetzenbaumLighter and more delicate; long shank, short bladeFine blunt dissection of delicate tissue
MayoHeavier, thicker bladesCutting tough tissue, fascia, sutures
Potts-SmithAngled blades — 25°, 45°, 60°Cardiovascular: arteriotomy / venotomy at a precise controlled angle, essential for anastomosis
"Metzenbaum = meek and minute; Mayo = massive." The pair is asked in reverse constantly — Metzenbaum is never the heavier one.

RetractorDistinguishing featureUse

4. Clamps & Grasping Forceps

ClampJaw designSpecific use
Kocher's (Ochsner's)Teeth at the tip + longitudinal serrations, ratchetGripping thyroid and tough fibrous tissue / aponeurosis
Bulldog clampSmall spring clamp, no ring handlesTemporary occlusion of small vessels (vascular surgery)
Doyen's bowel clampLong, blade-like, flexible, non-crushingOccludes viable bowel without injuring it
Duval lung clampTriangular fenestrated jawAtraumatically grasps lung tissue
DeBakey forcepsMultiple rows of fine non-crushing teethThe standard vascular tissue forceps — secure vessel-wall handling with minimal trauma
Green-ArmytageBroad flat haemostatic jaw conforming to thick uterine wallCaesarean section — clamps bleeding edges of the uterine incision
Bonney's myomectomy clampHeavy clamp applied across the broad ligamentMyomectomy — temporarily occludes the uterine arteries for a bloodless field; released after uterine repair
Vulsellum (tenaculum)Strong toothed jawsGrasps & manipulates the cervix / uterus — hysterectomy, D&C; firm grip on tough cervical tissue
Crush or not? Kocher & Allis crush → only on tissue being removed. Doyen, Duval, DeBakey, Babcock are non-crushing → on tissue you keep. Fenestrations and fine multiple teeth are the visual signature of "atraumatic".

5. Needle Holders & Suture Needles

5.1 Needle holders

HolderLockSpecial feature / use
Mayo-HegarRatchet lockGeneral surgery standard
Olsen-HegarRatchet lockCombines holding + cutting — built-in scissor blades, so no instrument swap to cut suture
CastroviejoHAS a lock — spring type with a catch lockOphthalmic microsurgery; it merely lacks the conventional finger-ring ratchet, so "no lock at all" is wrong
All needle holders have cross-hatched (criss-cross serrated) jaws — this grips the needle and stops it rotating.

5.2 Suture needle types

NeedlePoint / cutting edgeTissue
Round body (taper point)Smooth tip, no cutting edgeAtraumatic passage — viscera, bowel, vessels
Conventional cuttingEdge on the INNER (concave) curveSkin and tough tissue
Reverse cuttingEdge on the OUTER (convex) curveSkin — reduces tissue cut-out / tear-through
Taper-cutCutting tip + taper bodyTough fibrous tissue, fascia, calcified vessel
Which side is sharp? Conventional = concave (inner); Reverse = outeR (convex). Reverse cutting turns the sharp edge away from the wound edge, so the suture is less likely to cut out through skin.

6. Suction Tips

Suction tipDesignField of use
YankauerBulbous end with a single openingGeneral & oropharyngeal suction
Poole'sOuter sheath with multiple perforationsAbdominal surgery — the sheath stops omentum / bowel being drawn in and occluding the tip
FrazierAngled, narrow bore with a thumb hole for suction control; relatively straighter shaftNeurosurgery & ENT; clearing blood and secretions
ZoellnerLong thin shaft, angled tip, very fine calibreMiddle-ear microsurgery under the operating microscope — precise suction in a confined space
Reusability: suction tips are not all disposable — metal tips, especially Frazier and Zoellner, are reusable after sterilization.
Fine + angled + middle ear = Zoellner, not Frazier. Frazier is the straighter, wider-bore neuro/ENT tip.

7. Bone, Orthopaedic & Trauma

7.1 Fracture fixation devices

DevicePlacementIndication
External fixator (Ilizarov)Frame outside the limb, pins through boneCompound / open fracture stabilization; limb lengthening
Kuntscher nailWithin the medullary canal (intramedullary)Femoral shaft fracture
DHS (Dynamic Hip Screw)Lag screw + side plateThe standard for intertrochanteric fracture
Sterilization: trauma instruments ARE autoclaved, using large-capacity sterilizers. Their bulk does not exclude them from autoclaving.

7.2 Bone-holding forceps

ForcepsFeature
Lane's bone-holdingSerrated jaws + ratchet lock
Lowman's bone clampHolds bone fragments reduced while plating
Ferguson'sPointed tips — grips small / rounded bones
Bone-holding forceps are holding / grasping instruments — never cutting instruments. Bone is cut by saws, osteotomes, rongeurs and shears.

7.3 Bone cutting & scraping

InstrumentActionUse
Gigli wire sawFlexible twisted wire with serrated cutting edges, drawn back and forth by handlesPassed around the bone — craniotomy (neuro) and amputation (ortho)
Volkmann's spoon (curette)Sharp-edged spoon-shaped scoop, several sizesScrapes out granulation tissue, abscess wall, cyst lining, bone cavity (e.g. osteomyelitis)

8. Neurosurgery & Spinal

8.1 Craniotomy — instruments in procedural order

StepInstrumentPurpose
1Hudson's brace + perforator bit (D'Errico)Drills the initial burr holes in the skull
2Craniotome (or Gigli saw with guide & handles)Cuts bone between the burr holes to raise a removable bone flap
3Penfield dissectorsSeparate dura from bone
4Bone waxHaemostasis of bleeding bone edges
Assertion–Reason model answer: "A skull perforator creates initial burr holes" [true] because "those holes are the starting points from which the craniotome cuts between them to create a bone flap for access to the brain" [true, and it correctly explains the assertion] → both correct, and the reason IS the explanation.
Craniotomy "B–S–D–W": Burr hole → Saw between holes → Dura stripped → Wax the bone bleed.

8.2 Spinal & ventricular instruments

InstrumentFunction
Kerrison rongeurUp-biting nibbler with a footplate — precise removal of bone around the spinal cord (laminectomy)
Leksell rongeurHeavy double-action nibbler — bulk bone removal (spine, skull)
Cushing's ventricular needlePunctures the cerebral ventricle (ventriculostomy) to drain excess CSF — raised intracranial pressure, hydrocephalus
Laminectomy tray core: Kerrison rongeur + Leksell rongeur + Love nerve root retractor (+ Penfield dissectors, Cushing's brain spatula).

9. Thoracic & Cardiovascular

InstrumentKey design pointRole in thoracotomy
Finochietto rib spreaderSelf-retaining ratchet mechanismThe PRIMARY rib-SPREADING instrument — holds the ribs apart to give access to the thoracic cavity
Rib shears (rib cutter)Heavy angled blades, powerful enough for dense cortical boneCUTS and removes rib segments when access must be widened
"Fino spreads, shears shear." Also note: Balfour is abdominal, never thoracic — it is the standard distractor in rib-spreader questions.

Also on the thoracic / vascular trolley, detailed elsewhere: Potts-Smith scissors (angled arteriotomy, §2.2) · DeBakey forceps, bulldog clamp, Duval lung clamp (§4).

10. Obstetrics & Gynaecology

10.1 D&C — instruments in procedural order

Sims speculum → Vulsellum → Uterine sound → Hegar's dilators → Curette
StepInstrumentPurpose at that step
1Sims speculumVisualize the cervix
2VulsellumGrasp & steady the cervix
3Uterine soundMeasure the depth of the cavity
4Hegar's dilatorsProgressively dilate the cervical canal
5CuretteScrape the endometrium
Five S's of D&C: See → Steady → Sound → Stretch → Scrape.

10.2 Hegar's dilators

A graduated set of smooth cylindrical metal rods of increasing diameter used to progressively dilate the cervical canal (D&C, evacuation, hysteroscopy). Numbering = diameter in mm.
Range: size 1 (1 mm) → size ~24–26 (12–13 mm), each successive dilator 0.5 mm larger (half-millimetre steps).
"1 to 26 = 1 mm to 13 mm" — the top number is about double the top diameter in mm, because the steps are half a millimetre.

10.3 Specula

  • Sims speculum — double-ended, retracts the posterior vaginal wall; needs an assistant or a weight.
  • Cusco's speculum — bivalve, self-retaining; routine cervical examination.

Gynaecological clamps in §4: Vulsellum, Green-Armytage (LSCS), Bonney's myomectomy clamp. Abdominal exposure for hysterectomy: Balfour (§3.2).

15. Wrong-Tray Traps

Every "all EXCEPT" / "LEAST likely to be included" item works the same way: one instrument from an unrelated specialty is slipped into an otherwise correct tray list. Learn the intruders and the pattern collapses.

TrayGenuine contentsThe intruder — and where it really belongs
TonsillectomyBoyle-Davis gag, Eve's snare, Negus forcepsBone rongeur → mastoidectomy
VasectomyAllis forceps, ring-tipped vas-holding forceps, mosquitoBone rongeur → orthopaedic
LaminectomyKerrison rongeur, Love nerve root retractor, Leksell rongeurCusco's bivalve vaginal speculum → gynaecology
CraniotomyHudson's brace/perforator, Gigli saw, Penfield dissectors, bone waxHegar's dilators → gynaecology
Basic ear proceduresHartmann's forceps, Jobson-Horne probe, Siegle's speculumDCR set → ophthalmic (blocked tear duct)
Basic eye proceduresWire speculum, iris repositor, Vannas scissorsHohmann bone lever → orthopaedic
Strabismus surgeryMuscle hook, tenotomy scissors, calipersGigli saw → ortho / neuro bone saw
ENT examinationThudichum's speculum, Lack's depressor, Bull's eye lampCushing's brain spatula → neurosurgery
Cataract nucleus deliveryVectis (lens loop)Chalazion clamp, muscle hook, lacrimal probe → all ophthalmic but entirely different purposes
Three repeat offenders planted as intruders: bone rongeur (into ENT and urology trays), Hegar's dilators (into craniotomy), Cusco's speculum (into spine). If an orthopaedic or gynaecological instrument turns up in a list that is otherwise all one specialty, that is your answer.
