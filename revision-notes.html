  • ✔ Occlude bowel lumen during anastomosis.
  • ✔ Prevent spillage of bowel contents.
  • ✔ Gentle (atraumatic) handling of bowel wall.

4.2 Skin Hooks vs Forceps

Facial surgery → skin hooks (e.g., Gillies skin hook) preferred over forceps. Hooks engage skin with minimal crushing; forceps crush skin edges → poor scarring. Minimal trauma → better cosmetic outcome (cosmesis is paramount on the face).

8. Tracheostomy

8.1 The Tracheostomy Set

ComponentFunction
Tracheal hookHolds trachea steady and lifts/retracts it forward during the approach — NOT for dilating the stoma
Trousseau's tracheal dilatorThree-pronged; dilates and holds open the tracheal stoma for tube insertion. Does not cut, hold the tube, or suction
Tracheostomy tube + obturator / inner cannulaAirway maintenance; obturator aids insertion, inner cannula removable for cleaning

Emergency tracheostomy sequence: incision → strap muscles separated → tracheal hook stabilises & lifts trachea → tracheal window made between 2nd–3rd or 3rd–4th tracheal rings → Trousseau's dilator widens the stoma → tube inserted.

8.2 Tracheostomy Tubes

TubeMaterialFeatures
ShileySilicone / PVC plastic (disposable)Cuffed; removable inner cannula for cleaning. NOT metallic
PortexPVC plastic (not metal)Cuffed / uncuffed variants
Jackson'sMetalClassic metallic tracheostomy tube
Cuffed vs uncuffed: a cuffed tube is preferred for patients on mechanical ventilation — the inflated cuff seals the airway, preventing air leak around the tube (which would compromise ventilation) and preventing aspiration of oropharyngeal secretions into the lower airway.
Adult MALE cuffed tube ID = 7.0–8.0 mm  |  Adult FEMALE = 6–7 mm
Sizing balance: large enough for adequate ventilation, small enough to minimise tracheal wall pressure (ischaemia/stenosis). OD is always larger than ID.

12.2 Cataract Surgery — Instruments by Technique

InstrumentTechniqueFunction
Phaco handpiecePhacoemulsificationUltrasonic energy emulsifies the lens nucleus into fragments, then aspirates them. Frequency 28,000–40,000 Hz (28–40 kHz)
Capsulorrhexis forcepsPhacoContinuous curvilinear capsulorrhexis (CCC)
IOL folder / injectorPhacoFolds a flexible (foldable) IOL and injects it through a micro-incision (2.2–3.2 mm) into the capsular bag

Correct phaco sequence (foldable IOL): Keratome (incision) → Capsulorrhexis forceps (CCC) → Phaco handpiece (hydrodissection, emulsification, aspiration) → IOL folder/injector (implantation into capsular bag).
K-C-P-I = Keratome, Capsulorrhexis, Phaco, IOL. Foldable IOL exists because a small incision (2.2–3.2 mm) beats the large wound needed for rigid PMMA lenses.
Rigid vs foldable: rigid PMMA IOL needs a large incision; foldable IOL + injector allows micro-incision surgery → less astigmatism, faster recovery.
