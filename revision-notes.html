1.4 Occupied Bed-Making Sequence

Steps: Explain procedure (reduce anxiety, gain cooperation) → loosen linen → roll patient to one side (facing away from working side) → roll used bottom sheet toward patient → place clean bottom sheet + mackintosh + draw sheet on cleared half, tuck → roll patient over the bundle to clean side → remove soiled linen → pull clean linen through → tuck in. First action = explain + roll to one side.

2. Immobility, Pressure Sores & Skin Care

2.1 Pressure Ulcers (Decubitus / Bedsores)

MOST effective prevention = reposition every 2 h + skin inspection. Do NOT vigorously massage reddened areas (damages fragile capillaries); talcum cakes/irritates; same position CAUSES sores.
StageFeature
Stage 1Non-blanchable erythema, skin intact — needs immediate pressure relief
Stage 2Blister / shallow crater (partial skin loss)
Stage 3Full-thickness skin loss
Stage 4Muscle / bone exposed
Common sites (supine): sacrum (commonest), heels, elbows, occiput, scapulae. Lateral: greater trochanter, malleolus, ear. Bony prominences with little fat = most vulnerable.
Back care (back rub) techniqueLong firm strokes sacrum→upward with lotionCircular around bony points; AVOID reddened areas; warm lotion; 3–5 min; promotes venous return. No vigorous rubbing.

2.2 Complications of Prolonged Immobility

  • Pressure sores — sustained pressure → ischaemia.
  • DVT — venous stasis → clot; calf swelling + positive Homans' sign.
  • Hypostatic (chest) pneumonia — secretions pool in dependent lung.
  • Renal calculi — disuse osteoporosis → hypercalciuria → stones.
  • Constipation, urinary stasis, muscle wasting (atrophy), contractures, depression.
Immobility causes muscle WASTING & weakness — never increased strength. Early mobilisation + passive/active ROM always encouraged.

3. Sick Room, Hygiene & Nurse's Role

3.1 Ideal Sick Room

  • Well-ventilated — fresh air, no direct drafts on patient.
  • Quiet location; adequate natural light (cheerful, aids observation) with option to dim for rest — constant dim light is depressing & hinders observation.
  • Temp 18–20°C (65–68°F); humidity 40–60%; newborns/elderly slightly warmer (20–22°C).

3.2 Oral Hygiene

  • Give mouth care ≥ twice daily + after meals.
  • Unconscious: use moistened swabs/gauze on forceps (toothbrush = aspiration risk); position on SIDE (lateral).
  • Especially important when NPO / on IV — ↓ saliva → dry mouth, bacteria/fungi multiply → thrush.
White tongue patches + foul mouth + low urine (500 mL/day) in bedridden patientOral thrush (candidiasis) from dehydration + poor mouth careRx: ↑ fluids, mouth care ≥4-hrly, antifungal if prescribed, inform doctor.

3.3 Role & Ethics of the Nurse

  • Maintain accurate records (vitals, drugs given, intake/output); be cheerful & tactful; report changes to doctor.
  • Confidentiality — never discuss patient information with neighbours/anyone not involved in care.

4.2 BP Measurement (Correct Steps)

  • Patient rested 5 min, seated/lying comfortably — measure at REST (exercise falsely raises reading).
  • Cuff snug on bare arm at heart level; inflate 30 mmHg above estimated systolic (palpatory).
  • Deflate 2–3 mmHg/sec; first sound = systolic, disappearance = diastolic.

5.1 Fever Management & Patterns

High fever 40.2°C nursing careTepid sponge, light clothing, ↑ fluids, prescribed antipyreticTepid (not cold — cold → shivering → more heat); no heavy blankets; don't withhold fluids.
PatternFeatureExample
IntermittentReturns to normal ≥1×/dayMalaria, septicaemia
RemittentFluctuates >1°C, stays above normalMost infections
ContinuousConstant, <1°C fluctuationTyphoid, pneumonia
Not all fevers need antibiotics — viral fevers are self-limiting; treat the CAUSE.

5.2 Hot vs Cold Applications

6. Nutrition & Fluid Balance

6.1 Therapeutic Diets

DietContentsIndications
Clear liquidWater, broth, jelly, clear juicePost-op, pre-procedure, acute GI upset — FIRST diet after NPO
Low sodium (<2 g/day)—Hypertension, heart failure, renal oedema
High proteinEggs, meat, dairy, pulsesBurns, wounds, post-surgery, malnutrition
DiabeticControlled carbs, regular fixed-time mealsMatches insulin action; avoids hypo/hyperglycaemia
Post-op oral progression: sips of water → clear liquids → full liquids (milk, soup) → soft → normal. Clear liquids first: easy to digest, minimal residue, assess swallow/tolerance.
Elderly nutrition↓ calories, adequate protein, Ca + Vit D, fibre + fluidSmall frequent meals (5–6×) — large meals poorly tolerated. Protein prevents sarcopenia; Ca/D prevent osteoporosis.

6.2 Fluid Balance & Restriction

  • Record ALL intake (oral, IV, NG feeds) & ALL output (urine, vomit, drains, diarrhoea).
  • 1 kg weight gain ≈ 1 litre fluid retention. Needed in heart failure, renal disease, post-op, burns, oedema — not only ICU.
Restriction 1000 mL/day — how to divide~500 day / 300 evening / 200 night (50/30/20%)Bulk in day (active, meals, meds); offer ice chips; small cups; never withhold for meds.

7. Tube Feeding, Wounds & Asepsis

7.1 Nasogastric Tube

Confirm NG placement before feedAspirate + test pH (acidic 1–5.5)'Whoosh'/auscultation test unreliable (no longer recommended). X-ray = gold standard. Length marking detects migration only.
NG feeding position30–45° Fowler's during + 30 min afterPrevents aspiration/reflux. Check position each feed; room-temp feed; flush water before & after.

7.2 Wound Healing

TypeFeature
Primary intentionClean edges sutured together; fast, minimal scar (surgical incision)
Secondary intentionLarge/infected wound heals from base by granulation; bigger scar, slower
Tertiary (delayed primary)Left open (contaminated), cleaned over days, then closed surgically
DehiscenceWound breaks open after closure (complication)
Wound dehiscence / evisceration: partial/total separation of layers; risk in debilitated patients. NEVER push exposed viscera back — cover with sterile saline-soaked dressing & take to OT.

7.3 Asepsis & Sharps

Aseptic (no-touch) techniqueSterile method preventing microbe entry to woundSterile kit, hand decontamination, non-touch, clean→dirty, sterile field. For dressings, catheter, injections.
Surgical drainTube draining blood/pus/fluid from op sitePenrose, Jackson-Pratt, Hemovac — prevents haematoma/seroma.
Sharps / needle disposalStraight into puncture-proof sharps box, NO recappingRecapping = commonest needlestick cause. Never bend/break; seal at ¾ full.
Butterfly strip (Steri-Strip)Adhesive strips holding wound edges togetherSuture alternative for superficial, clean, linear wounds. Not for deep/tense/contaminated wounds or joints.

10. Oxygen Therapy

  • Indications: cyanosis (bluish lips/nails), severe dyspnoea, suspected MI, shock, CO poisoning. Mild common cold does NOT need O₂.
  • O₂ is a drug — give per physician's order; unnecessary use is harmful (COPD, retinopathy of prematurity).
  • Supports combustion (fire hazard — no smoking/flames); requires humidification.
  • Flow rate depends on condition/device — NOT always 15 L/min. High flow can suppress respiratory drive in COPD.

11. Drug Administration & Drug Laws

11.1 Routes — Speed of Onset

IV (sec) > inhalation > sublingual (1–2 min) > IM (10–20) > SC (15–30) > oral (30–90) > rectal > topical
Fastest routeIV — direct to bloodstream, 100% bioavailability
IM site in infantsVastus lateralis (anterolateral thigh)Well-developed at birth, no major nerve/vessel. Dorsogluteal avoided <3 yrs (sciatic nerve risk).

11.2 Safe Administration & Refusal

  • Check drug label 3 times (from shelf, when pouring, before giving); never give drugs prepared by others; document AFTER giving (documenting before = falsification).
  • Patients have the RIGHT to refuse — forcing is assault/illegal. Document refusal, inform doctor.
  • Mental-illness refusal: FIRST calmly explain importance & offer again later; explore reason; document; inform doctor. Hidden meds = illegal; restraint = last resort.

11.3 Indian Drug Schedules & Laws

Drug / ScheduleControl
Schedule X — morphine, diazepamNarcotic/psychotropic; strictest, double-lock, register
Schedule H — amoxicillinPrescription-only; no OTC
Schedule H1Subset of H — pharmacist keeps register (prescriber, patient, drug, qty, date); antibiotics, anti-TB, habit-forming (curbs resistance)
Schedule K / OTC — paracetamolNo prescription needed
NDPS Act1985 — Narcotic Drugs & Psychotropic SubstancesControls manufacture/sale/use; strict penalties. Works with Drugs & Cosmetics Act 1940.

12. Infection, Immunity & Immunisation

12.1 Diseases & Causative Organisms

DiseaseOrganismSpread
CholeraVibrio choleraeFaecal-oral (water); rice-water diarrhoea. Incubation few hrs–5 days
TyphoidSalmonella typhiFaecal-oral; NOT mosquito-borne
Hepatitis BHBVBlood / sexual / mother-to-child; NOT waterborne (Hep A & E are)
MalariaPlasmodiumFemale Anopheles mosquito; cyclical fever + chills
Typhoid course: Wk1 step-ladder fever, coated tongue, relative bradycardia; Wk2 sustained fever, rose spots (abdomen), hepatosplenomegaly. Dx blood/stool culture (Widal supportive). Rx bed rest (prevents perforation), high-calorie soft diet; tepid sponging IS allowed.
Transmission routes: Direct contact (touch, sexual, droplet <1 m) · Airborne (nuclei <5 µm — TB, measles, chickenpox) · Vehicle (food/water = faecal-oral; blood; fomites) · Vector (mosquito=malaria, flea=plague). Hep B: blood-borne; HCWs at risk (needlestick); 3-dose vaccine (0,1,6 mo).

14. Shock & Haemorrhage

14.1 Types of Shock

ShockCause
HypovolaemicFluid / blood loss
CardiogenicPump failure (MI) → ↑CVP; fluids CONTRAINDICATED (worsen pulmonary oedema); Rx inotropes + diuretics
AnaphylacticAllergic vasodilation + ↑ permeability + bronchospasm; Rx epinephrine
NeurogenicLoss of sympathetic tone (spinal injury)
SepticEarly: warm, flushed skin, bounding pulse; tachycardia + hypotension (cold skin = late)

14.2 Hypovolaemic Shock — Signs

  • Cold, clammy, pale skin (vasoconstriction); rapid weak thready pulse; restlessness (cerebral hypoperfusion); tachypnoea (RR ↑, not ↓).
  • Earliest sign = tachycardia (>100 bpm); hypotension, anuria, cyanosis are LATE (decompensation).
  • Trendelenburg (legs up 20°) → ↑ venous return/preload.

14.3 Blood Loss Estimation

Total blood volume ≈ 70 mL/kg  |  Min urine output 0.5 mL/kg/hr
Worked: 70 kg adult → ~5000 mL blood; 1500 mL loss = 30% (Class III → transfusion).   60 kg, Class II (15–30%): 4200 mL × 0.15–0.30 = 630–1260 mL (crystalloid).   Post-op urine, 60 kg × 8 h × 0.5 = 240 mL minimum (anuria 8 h → investigate).

15. Blood & Transfusion

15.1 Groups, Storage, Rate

Universal RBC donorO negative (no A, B, Rh)Safe alternative for any group in emergency (e.g. B+ patient). AB+ = universal plasma donor.
Whole blood / packed RBC storage2–6°C (shelf 35–42 days)Freezing destroys RBCs; room temp → bacteria.
Donation unit volume350–450 mL whole blood
Transfusion rate — first 15 minSlow 20–30 drops/minWatch for reactions; most acute reactions occur early.

15.2 Blood Components

  • FFP — all clotting factors; stored ≤ −18°C.
  • Platelets — 20–24°C, continuous agitation, shelf life 5 days.
  • Cryoprecipitate — Factor VIII, fibrinogen, vWF, Factor XIII.
  • Packed RBCs — 2–6°C, NOT frozen.

15.3 Transfusion Reactions

Acute haemolytic reaction (ABO mismatch) — MOST dangerous, fatal in minutes. Fever + chills + low back pain (renal capsule distension) within minutes → DIC, renal failure. (Febrile reaction lacks back pain; allergic = urticaria; TRALI = respiratory distress.)

16. Peri-operative & Post-op Care

16.1 Pre-operative

  • Obtain informed written consent; remove prosthetics/jewellery; record baseline vitals; give pre-meds in the ward (not after shifting to OT).
  • NPO 6–8 h before surgery → empty stomach → prevents aspiration (Mendelson syndrome) under GA.
  • Skin prep (shaving) reduces bacterial count at op site; done close to surgery to avoid micro-abrasion colonisation.
WHO Surgical Safety Checklist — Sign In (before induction): confirm identity/site/procedure · anaesthesia machine & pulse oximeter check · allergies · airway/aspiration risk · blood-loss risk. Instrument/sponge count = Sign Out.
