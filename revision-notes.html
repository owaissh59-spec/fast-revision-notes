2. Grasping / Holding Forceps

2.1 Master Comparison — Jaw Design Decides Everything

ForcepsJaw / tip designTypeUses
Kocher'sTransverse serrations + 1×2 teeth at tip; ratchet lockCrushing (traumatic)Tough tissue, rectus sheath, aponeurosis; clamping cystic duct in cholecystectomy
AllisShort interlocking sharp teeth (multiple)Crushing (traumatic)Fascia, aponeurosis, tough / to-be-excised tissue
BabcockFenestrated oval ring, NO teeth, smooth rounded jawsNon-crushing (atraumatic)Bowel, appendix, fallopian tube, ureter
Doyen bowel clampLongitudinal serrations + flexible hinge joint; long bladesNon-crushing intestinal clampOcclude bowel lumen in intestinal anastomosis / resection
Lane'sInterlocking teethCrushingTissue traction
Lahey (thyroid tenaculum)Sharp interlocking teethCrushing / tractionGrasp thyroid tissue, tough fibrous tissue, traction on lobe. NEVER as artery forceps
Spencer WellsTransverse serrations, box joint, ratchetCrushing — artery forcepsHemostasis (crushes the vessel)

Teeth = Trauma. Any tip with teeth (Kocher, Allis, Lane, Lahey, Negus) is crushing. Any tip that is smooth / fenestrated / longitudinally serrated (Babcock, Doyen) is non-crushing.
Longitudinal = Lenient; Transverse = Terminates tissue. Doyen's longitudinal serrations spare the bowel wall; transverse serrations crush.

2.2 Allis vs Babcock — the classic viva pair

FeatureAllisBabcock
TipInterlocking sharp teethSmooth fenestrated (open) oval loop
TraumaTraumatic / crushingAtraumatic / non-crushing
Used forFascia, tough tissueBowel, appendix, tube, ureter
Basic design & lockIdentical — same shank shape, same ratchet locking mechanism. Only the jaw tip differs.
Exam trap: the statement "both share the same basic design and identical ratchet lock" is TRUE — don't reject it. Only the jaw tip differs.

3. Hemostatic Forceps & Towel Clips

3.1 Ratchet Mechanism

Spencer Wells / standard hemostat = 3 ratchet positions (clicks). 1st click = gentle hold · 2nd = moderate · 3rd = maximum clamping force on the vessel.
"Three clicks to clamp" — 3 is the number for hemostat ratchets (do not confuse with blade or fork numbers).

3.2 Towel Clips & Draping

ClipDesignPoint
Backhaus (towel forceps)Cross-action, sharp penetrating tipsMost commonly used to secure drapes; pierces drape + superficial skin
EdnaNon-penetrating, bluntReduces needle-stick / skin injury & sharps risk
Exam trap: a "Jones towel clip = cross-action type" style statement is FALSE — cross-action design refers to Backhaus itself. Beware non-standard classifications inserted as distractors. Allis / Kocher's / Babcock are tissue forceps, never draping clips.

Only "B" is self-retaining: BALFOUR. Deaver, Langenbeck, Green's, Kilner, Desmarres are ALL hand-held. Deaver's biggest exam trap is being mislabelled "self-retaining".
Deaver = Deep & curveD; Langenbeck = L-shaped.

4.2 Skin Hooks vs Tissue Forceps (facial surgery)

Skin hooks are preferred for skin flaps in facial surgery because they pierce only a single point of skin → minimal trauma, dermal blood supply preserved → less wound-edge necrosis. Tissue forceps crush tissue between jaws and compress the dermal vasculature — unacceptable where cosmesis is paramount.

5.2 Mayo vs Metzenbaum Scissors

FeatureMayoMetzenbaum
BuildHeavier, shorter bladesLighter, more delicate, long shank & short blades
CutsTough tissue & suturesDelicate tissue — peritoneum, fascia, blunt dissection
VariantsBoth available curved and straight
"Metzenbaum = Mild; Mayo = Muscular." Trap: "Metzenbaum is heavier than Mayo" is FALSE — it is the lighter one.

5.3 Gigli Saw

  • Flexible twisted-wire saw operated with two T-shaped handles.
  • Cuts in BOTH directions — on forward AND backward strokes → efficient. (The "cuts only in one direction" statement is FALSE.)
  • Craniotomy: passed between skull and dura via burr holes, then sawn to cut bone between them.
  • Amputation: cutting long bones. Its flexibility lets it be passed around bone.
"Gigli Goes both ways, Guided by two handles, through burr holes."

5.4 Periosteal Elevator

  • Strips periosteum off bone. Typically one sharp end (initiate dissection) + one blunt end (elevate).
  • Howarth type = the ENT workhorse (nasal / sinus surgery).
  • Frequently used in septal surgery (septoplasty / SMR) to elevate mucoperiosteal flaps — the statement "not used in septal surgery" is FALSE.

6. Needle Holders & Suturing

FeatureMayo-HegarCastroviejo
LockRatchet lockSpring-action locking mechanism
JawsCross-hatched jawsFine microsurgical jaws
FieldGeneral surgeryOphthalmic / microsurgery — fine needles 6-0 to 10-0
Needle typesAll standardBoth cutting AND round-bodied microsurgical needles — NOT round-bodied only
Grip the needle at its MIDPOINT or POSTERIOR THIRD — never at the tip (blunts it) or the swage (bends/breaks it).
Tungsten carbide inserts in the jaws improve grip and prolong instrument life.
Length rule: needle holder must be longer than the wound depth by ≥ 2–3 cm. Too short in a deep wound → hands obstruct the view, imprecise suturing.
Castroviejo = spring + fine (6-0→10-0); Mayo-Hegar = ratchet + cross-hatched. The spring action exists because ophthalmic suturing needs extreme precision and it reduces hand fatigue in long microsurgical cases.

7. Suction & Diathermy

7.1 Yankauer vs Frazier Suction

FeatureYankauerFrazier
TipRigid, bulbous tip with multiple small holes (prevents tissue aspiration)Small diameter, angled tip
ControlNo thumbholeThumbhole → precise control of suction pressure
Used inOropharyngeal suctioning; abdominal surgeryENT & neurosurgery — narrow fields, near delicate neural structures
"Yankauer = Yawning mouth (oropharynx, bulky). Frazier = Fine, aNgled, thumbhole → Neuro/ENT." Yankauer is too bulky for confined spaces.

7.2 Diathermy

AspectFact
MonopolarRequires a patient return electrode (grounding pad); current traverses the patient
BipolarCurrent passes only between the two forceps tips → safer near nerves; no grounding pad needed
CUTTING modeContinuous, undamped waveform
COAGULATION modeInterrupted (pulsed / damped) waveform → more heat, seals vessels
"Cut = Continuous · Coag = Choppy." Trap: "coagulation uses a continuous waveform" is FALSE.

Why Bowman's probes are graduated: the nasolacrimal duct differs in diameter between infants and adults, and probing requires progressive dilation — so the graduated series exists to match duct calibre. (Valid Assertion–Reason pair: R explains A.)

8.2 Eye Speculum

  • Wire speculum (Barraquer's): keeps eyelids open during surgery; self-retaining by spring tension; available in adult and paediatric sizes.
  • Design is WIRE, not a solid blade. The solid-blade lid instrument is the Desmarres retractor — a different instrument.

8.3 Chalazion Clamp (Desmarres chalazion forceps)

FeatureDetail
StructureRing on the skin side + solid plate on the conjunctival side, joined by a horizontal limb with a screw

"Ring on skin, Plate + cut on Conjunctiva." The commonest trap is claiming the incision is made on the skin side.

10. ENT — Ear

InstrumentDesignUse
Aural speculumCone-shaped, multiple sizesStraightens the external canal → visualise tympanic membrane & EAC; used with otoscope head / headlight; wax removal
Jobson Horne probeDouble-ended: ring/loop one end + curette/scoop the otherRing → carry/remove cotton-wool pledgets; curette → remove ear wax & debris. Standard in the ear examination set
Myringotomy knifeSmall lancet- or sickle-shaped ANGLED bladeIncise the tympanic membrane through an aural speculum. NOT a long straight scalpel-like blade — that could not work down the narrow canal
Tuning fork — 512 HzStandard clinical forkRinne & Weber tests — differentiate conductive vs sensorineural hearing loss
Why 512 Hz? Optimal balance of audibility vs decay time. 128 / 256 Hz → too much tactile vibration (patient feels rather than hears → confusion). 1024 Hz → decays too quickly for clinical testing.
"512 — one fork, two tests (Rinne + Weber)." Low forks are felt; high forks fade.

12. Thyroid Instruments

InstrumentRole in thyroidectomy
Lahey forceps (thyroid tenaculum)Sharp interlocking teeth — grasp thyroid tissue, hold tough fibrous tissue, give traction on the lobe during mobilisation. NOT an artery forceps (teeth would damage vessel walls)

Thyroid trio = Lahey (grab) · Kocher dissector (separate) · Green's (retract — see §4). Note Kocher lends his name to BOTH the toothed crushing forceps and the blunt thyroid dissector — read the stem carefully.
