1. Urinary Catheters & Drainage

1.1 Foley Catheter — Core Facts

AttributeFactNote
TypeSelf-retaining catheterRetention by inflatable balloon at tip
Balloon volume (adult)10 mL sterile water30 mL for 3-way / haematuria catheters; use water, not air/saline
SizingFrench gauge (Fr)1 Fr = 0.33 mm outer circumference-based
2-wayDrainage + balloonRoutine catheterisation
3-wayDrainage + balloon + irrigationContinuous bladder irrigation, esp. post-TURP
"Ten for two, thirty for three" — 2-way Foley balloon = 10 mL; 3-way haematuria catheter balloon = 30 mL.

1.2 Suprapubic Catheter (SPC)

Route of insertion Percutaneous, through anterior abdominal wall above pubic symphysis into a DISTENDED bladder Bladder must be full — it rises out of the pelvis and displaces peritoneum, so the tract avoids bowel.

Indications (= reason the urethral route is bypassed): urethral trauma/rupture • tight urethral stricture • prostatic obstruction where urethral catheterisation fails or is contraindicated.

1.3 Urinary Drainage vs Pleural Drainage — Don't Confuse

Underwater seal drainage (UWSD) = pleural cavity only (air/fluid). It is NOT for bladder, peritoneum, or pericardium. Bladder drainage = catheter; pericardium = pericardiocentesis/drain.

2. Urethral Dilators & Bladder Stone Instruments

2.1 Dilatation of Urethral Stricture

InstrumentDescriptionUse / Notes
Metal sounds (bougies)Smooth curved metal, graduated sizes in Fr; curve follows male urethraRoutine dilatation of urethral stricture

2.2 Bladder Stone — Lithotrite & Litholapaxy

Lithotrite — structure & action Transurethral instrument with TWO blades (one fixed, one mobile) that mechanically CRUSHES a bladder stone intravesically Purely mechanical and intravesical — NOT extracorporeal shock waves (that is ESWL, a different modality).
TermMeaning
LitholapaxyCrushing a bladder stone with a lithotrite + evacuating the fragments. Blind or optical.
LithotripsyFragmentation by an energy source (laser, ultrasound, pneumatic, ESWL)
CystolithotomyOpen surgical removal of bladder stone
NephrolithotomyOpen removal of renal stone (uses Randall stone forceps)
-lapaxy = "wash out" → crush and evacuate. -tripsy = "rub/grind" → energy-based fragmentation.

2.3 Randall Stone Forceps

Randall stone forceps Grasp & extract renal/ureteric stones during OPEN nephrolithotomy Come in graded angles (1st, 2nd, 3rd, 4th) to reach different calyces. NOT a nephrectomy instrument.

3. Urological Endoscopes & TURP

3.2 Cystoscope — Details

  • Available rigid and flexible.
  • Rigid cystoscope uses the Hopkins rod-lens system → superior image quality.
  • Operative cystoscopy permits biopsy and fulguration of bladder tumours.
  • Distension medium = sterile saline or water. Not CO2 — CO2 is the laparoscopy gas.

3.3 TURP Irrigation Fluid

Monopolar TURP → glycine 1.5%. Chosen because it is a non-electrolyte, non-conducting solution, so it does not disperse/short-circuit the monopolar diathermy current → effective cutting and coagulation. Bipolar TURP → normal saline is safe (current travels only between the two electrodes on the loop), which greatly reduces TUR syndrome risk.
"Mono = no ions; Bi = saline is fine."

6. Thoracic Clamps, Endoscopy & Pleural Biopsy

6.1 Lung / Bronchus Clamps

ClampDesignUse
Duval lung clampTriangular, fenestrated, serrated jawsAtraumatic grasping of lung tissue — the triangular shape spreads pressure over a wide area so delicate parenchyma is not crushed (this IS the correct explanation of its design)
Price-Thomas clampBronchus/lung clampPneumonectomy / lobectomy — bronchial stump control

Distractor to watch: the Satinsky is a vascular, not a lung, clamp (§8.1).

6.2 VATS (Video-Assisted Thoracoscopic Surgery)

  • Minimally invasive thoracic surgery via thoracoscope + specialised instruments through small port incisions.
  • Requires single-lung ventilation — the operative lung is collapsed to create working space.
  • VATS lobectomy IS established for early-stage lung cancer. (The classic false statement is "VATS cannot be used for lobectomy".)

6.3 Abrams Needle

Abrams needle Closed PLEURAL biopsy — obtains parietal pleural tissue High yield in tuberculous pleurisy and mesothelioma. Not a lung-parenchymal or bone biopsy needle.

7. Chest Drain (UWSD) & Pericardiocentesis

7.1 Intercostal Drain — Technique Rules

RuleDetailWhy
Site"Triangle of safety", 5th intercostal spaceBorders: ant. border of latissimus dorsi • lat. border of pectoralis major • base = horizontal line at the level of the nipple / 5th ICS
Level within the spaceInsert just above the UPPER border of the ribAvoids the neurovascular bundle which runs in the groove along the lower border of the rib above
Bottle positionUnderwater seal bottle kept BELOW chest levelGravity drainage; prevents fluid siphoning back into the pleural space. (The classic false statement says "above chest level".)
"Above the rib, below the chest" — needle/drain passes above the rib's upper border; the bottle stays below the patient's chest.

7.2 UWSD — Purpose, Components, Indications

AspectContent
PurposeDrain air or fluid from the pleural cavity (pneumothorax, haemothorax, empyema). The water seal is a one-way valve preventing air re-entry.
Components (modern 3-chamber unit)1 Collection chamber • 2 Water-seal chamber (one-way valve) • 3 Suction-control chamber (regulates negative pressure) • 4 Pressure-relief valve (safety). All four are present.
IndicationsTension pneumothorax (after needle decompression) • massive haemothorax • empyema thoracis
NOT an indicationSimple rib fracture without pneumo/haemothorax → conservative: analgesia

7.3 Pericardiocentesis

ItemCorrect detail
IndicationDrain pericardial effusion / cardiac tamponade
Standard approachSubxiphoid (Marfan's) — needle between xiphoid process and left costal margin, directed towards the left shoulder
MonitoringECG — ST elevation appears if the needle touches myocardium (withdraw slightly)

9. Orthopaedics — Pins, Wires, Nails & Fixators

9.1 Percutaneous Pins & Wires

DeviceDiameterPurpose
Steinmann pin3–6 mm (thick)Skeletal traction — inserted through bone: tibial tuberosity, calcaneum, olecranon
Kirschner wire (K-wire)0.7–1.5 mm (thin)Temporary fixation of small fragments
External fixator—Percutaneous pins connected by external bars/frame
External fixator in Grade IIIB open fracture (A + R both true, R explains A): preferred over internal fixation because it stabilises the fracture without placing implants in a contaminated wound, minimises further soft-tissue/periosteal stripping, preserves remaining blood supply, and leaves the wound accessible for dressings and flap coverage.

10. Orthopaedics — Plates: DCP / LC-DCP / LCP

PlateDefining featureBiology / mechanics
DCP (Dynamic Compression Plate)Oval/oblong holes; eccentrically tightened screw slides → axial compression at fracture siteFull plate-bone contact. No screw-head–plate locking mechanism — that is the LCP feature (classic "NOT a feature of DCP" answer).
LC-DCP (Limited Contact DCP)Undercuts on the undersurface reduce bone–plate contact areaPreserves periosteal blood supply, reduces cortical porosis. Provides the SAME compression as DCP — not less.
LCP (Locking Compression Plate)Threaded holes — screw head locks into the plateActs as an internal fixator / fixed-angle construct; no need to press plate onto bone
"LC = Less Contact, same Compression. Locking head = LCP, not DCP."

13.2 Craniotomy — Instruments & Sequence

Bone-flap sequence: 1 Make burr holes with Hudson brace + perforator → 2 Strip dura off the inner table between holes with a dural separator → 3 Connect burr holes with Gigli saw / craniotome → 4 Elevate the bone flap.
"Bore → Separate → Connect → Lift" — dura must be freed before the saw passes, or you tear it.

13.3 CSF Diversion

SystemComponents / target
VP shunt3 parts: ventricular (proximal) catheter in lateral ventricle • reservoir/valve (Pudenz, Hakim) regulating CSF flow • peritoneal (distal) catheter.
A Fogarty balloon is NOT a shunt component — it is an embolectomy catheter.
EVD (external ventricular drain)Tip in the frontal horn of the lateral ventricle — relatively avascular, reliable drainage. Entry at Kocher's point: 11 cm posterior to nasion, 3 cm lateral to midline.

15.1 Obstetric Forceps — Which, When, Why

ForcepsDistinguishing designIndication
Wrigley'sShort shank (and short handles)Outlet / lift-out delivery — head already visible at the perineum, so less force is applied; also for the head at LSCS (R explains A)

15.2 Ventouse (Vacuum Extractor)

  • Applies traction by creating negative pressure on the fetal scalp.
  • Complications: chignon (artificial caput, resolves) and cephalhaematoma.
  • Kiwi OmniCup = handheld, single-use vacuum device.
  • Contraindicated before full dilatation (10 cm) — risks cervical injury/annular detachment. (This is the classic false statement.)
Ventouse = "full dilatation only." Forceps ± ventouse both need a fully dilated cervix; only Wrigley's at LSCS escapes the rule.

15.4 The Retractor Axis Most Often Examined

Self-retaining (holds itself, no assistant): Finochietto · Gil-Vernet · Leyla · Cusco's speculum · Millin's.
Hand-held (needs an assistant): Cushing brain spatula · Langenbeck · DeLee · Doyen · Sims' speculum.
The two classic swaps: Leyla vs Cushing (neuro) and Cusco's vs Sims' (gynae) — in each pair the first is self-retaining, the second is not.

16. Clinical Scenarios & Troubleshooting

16.1 Post-TURP Clot Retention

Scenario: 65 y male, post-TURP, 3-way catheter on continuous irrigation; drainage stops / output falls, bladder distends. → Immediate manual bladder washout with a catheter-tip (Toomey) syringe to aspirate and flush out the clots.
Why not the others: increasing the irrigation rate will not clear existing clots and worsens bladder distension • removing the catheter is wrong (the problem is clot obstruction, not malposition) • return to theatre is reserved for uncontrollable bleeding after washout fails.

16.2 Chest Drain — Continuous Bubbling

Scenario: Bubbling in the underwater seal during both inspiration and expiration, no fluid drainage, ongoing dyspnoea and reduced air entry. → Persistent air leak: either a bronchopleural fistula or a drain side-hole lying outside the pleural cavity sucking in atmospheric air. Continuous (not swinging/intermittent) bubbling with a non-resolving pneumothorax is the giveaway.
Swinging = good, bubbling on cough = normal air leak, CONTINUOUS bubbling = pathological leak or a mispositioned drain.
